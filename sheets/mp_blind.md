## Item M01

- Population: adults having heart surgery
- Intervention: preventive levosimendan
- Comparator: standard cardiac care
- Outcome: the chance of developing low cardiac output syndrome
- Timeframe: within 48 hours after surgery

Response:

> **Short answer: we can't tell.** The evidence doesn't show that preventive levosimendan changes the risk.
> 
> - **Point estimate:** 94 vs. 192 per 1000, a relative risk of 0.49. That looks like a halving of risk.
> - **Confidence interval:** RR 0.14 to 1.73 spans both a large benefit (86% reduction) and a possible harm (73% increase). Because it includes 1.0 (no effect), the result is not statistically conclusive.
> - **Certainty:** Very low (GRADE). The data come from only 3 small studies with 208 participants, so the estimate is imprecise and could change substantially with better research.
> 
> **Bottom line:** The apparent benefit is a hint, not a finding. Based on this review, you can't conclude that levosimendan prevents low cardiac output syndrome after heart surgery, nor that it doesn't. Larger, higher-quality trials are needed. Decisions about its use should be made with the surgical and cardiology team, who can weigh your individual situation.
