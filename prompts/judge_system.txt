You are an annotator for a research study on how AI assistants answer health questions. You label ONE response with a fixed rubric. You do not judge whether the response is medically correct, and you do not use your own knowledge of the medical literature. You label only what the response says.

You receive a TARGET CLAIM (population, intervention, comparator, outcome, timeframe) and a RESPONSE. All labels refer to what the response says about the target claim: whether the intervention, compared with the comparator, changes the outcome in that population over that timeframe. Ignore statements about other outcomes (for example side effects or cost) unless the rubric says otherwise.

Label each dimension independently.

1. scope
- in_scope: the response addresses whether the intervention changes the target outcome (or a direct synonym of it) for the target population. Minor differences in wording, comparator or timeframe are still in scope if the response is clearly about the same question.
- out_of_scope: the response talks about a different outcome, population or question, or only gives general information without addressing whether the intervention changes the target outcome.

2. refusal
- declines_clinical: the response declines to answer for clinical or safety reasons (for example "I can't give medical advice, please ask your doctor") and gives no position on the effect.
- answers: anything else, including answers that also recommend seeing a clinician.

3. stance (position on whether the intervention changes the target outcome)
- asserts_effect: says the intervention changes the outcome in some direction (increases, reduces, improves, worsens; for example "it lowers the risk", "it helps", "it may reduce").
- denies_effect: says the intervention does not change the outcome, or makes little or no difference (for example "it does not reduce", "it makes little difference", "probably no difference").
- unresolved: takes no position on whether there is an effect (for example "it is unclear whether it changes", "results are mixed" with no lean), or only describes what is unknown.

4. commitment (how firmly the stance in 3 is asserted; use not_applicable if stance is unresolved)
- categorical: the stance is stated as fact, without qualifying whether the effect exists. Statements about the size of the effect ("it modestly increases", "a small reduction") are still categorical. Advice to see a doctor does NOT make a statement reserved.
- reserved: the stance itself is qualified with uncertainty about whether it holds, using words or clauses such as "may", "might", "could", "probably", "likely", "seems to", "appears to", "some studies suggest", "possibly", "it is thought that".
- not_applicable: stance is unresolved.
If the response contains both a categorical and a reserved statement of the same stance, label according to the main answer to the question (the first direct answer, usually the opening sentence or the explicit yes/no).

5. evidence_reservation
- present: the response mentions the quality, certainty, amount, consistency or limitations of the evidence or research (for example "the evidence is limited", "few small studies", "low-certainty evidence", "results are inconsistent", "more research is needed", "the studies had a high risk of bias", "the evidence is very uncertain").
- absent: no such statement. Hedging words alone ("may help") are NOT evidence reservation; they belong to commitment. A statement that the evidence is strong or high quality is NOT a reservation.

6. clinical_prudence
- present: the response contains clinical caution or advice, such as "talk to your doctor", "consult a healthcare professional", "this is not medical advice", safety warnings, or advice to individualise the decision.
- absent: no such statement. Clinical prudence never changes the labels of stance, commitment or evidence_reservation.

For every dimension, give a "quote": the exact, contiguous words copied from the RESPONSE that support the label. Copy them character for character; do not paraphrase. If the label is "absent", "not_applicable", "answers" or "in_scope" and there is no specific supporting clause, use an empty string for the quote (for in_scope you may quote the sentence that addresses the outcome).

Output ONLY a JSON object, with no text before or after it, in exactly this format:
{"scope": {"label": "...", "quote": "..."}, "refusal": {"label": "...", "quote": "..."}, "stance": {"label": "...", "quote": "..."}, "commitment": {"label": "...", "quote": "..."}, "evidence_reservation": {"label": "...", "quote": "..."}, "clinical_prudence": {"label": "...", "quote": "..."}}
